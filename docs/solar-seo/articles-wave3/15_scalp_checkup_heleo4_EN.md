*Most people who worry about their hair look at the ends: split, dry, falling out on the pillow. Yet every hair is grown by the skin it sits in. Scalp skin is skin, with sebaceous glands, a microbiome, a barrier and its own reaction to Dubai's water, sun and air conditioning. ## Why the scalp comes first

A hair follicle is a small organ buried 3-5 mm deep in the skin, fed by dermal capillaries and surrounded by sebaceous glands. If the skin around it is inflamed, clogged with sebum and dead cells, or dehydrated with a broken barrier, the follicle grows a thinner, shorter, weaker hair. No mask on the lengths fixes that, because the problem is 3 mm below the surface.

Two things make this especially relevant to residents of Dubai:

- **Desalinated, hard tap water and pool chlorine.** Chlorine is an oxidising agent that strips sebum and roughens the hair cuticle; dermatologists call the severe version pool dermatitis. Mineral deposits from hard water leave the scalp dull and tight, and local surveys quoted in the UAE press link scalp dryness to hard water in a large share of residents.

- **Sun, heat and constant air conditioning.** Summer UV pushes more follicles into the resting (telogen) phase, and 3-4 months later those hairs fall out, which is why shedding peaks in October and November, just as the outdoor season starts. Indoors, dry conditioned air adds dehydration and flaking.

Add stress, low vitamin D despite the sun, and nutritional gaps, and you get a typical Dubai scalp: oily at the roots, flaky, sensitive, with hair that looks thinner every year. Which factors apply to you is what a check-up is for.

## Step one: trichoscopy, the scalp under magnification

Trichoscopy is a non-invasive examination of the scalp with a digital dermatoscope at 60x to 200x magnification. It takes 15-20 minutes, needs only clean, unstyled hair, and shows what the eye cannot:

| What we look at | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Hair density per cm² | Baseline to compare with after the course |
| Hair shaft diameter and its variation | Variation above 20% in men and 10% in women (anisotrichosis) is the earliest sign of androgenetic thinning; in a 2024 systematic review of 2,860 participants it was present in 94% of cases |
| Vellus (miniaturised) hairs | Hairs under 0.03 mm thick and 2-3 mm long show follicles that are shrinking |
| Follicular openings | Yellow dots are openings blocked with sebum and keratin, white dots are empty follicles, brown halos around the follicle (peripilar sign) show inflammation |
| Scalp surface | Scaling, redness, sebum level, signs of seborrheic dermatitis or barrier damage |

The result is a map, not a guess. Congestion, seborrhea and inflammation without miniaturisation call for the cosmetology protocol below. Miniaturisation, empty follicles or scarring mean a referral to a trichologist or dermatologist before any aesthetic work.

## Step two: scalp peeling

A professional scalp peel uses acids (salicylic, glycolic, lactic, azelaic) or enzymes to dissolve the layer of dead cells, hardened sebum and mineral deposits that blocks the follicular openings.

- **Salicylic acid** is lipid-soluble, so it goes into the sebaceous duct and works as a keratolytic with mild anti-inflammatory action. In a 2024 cohort study, a salicylic acid based scalp regimen reduced dandruff scores from 2.45 to 1.10 over four weeks.
- **Glycolic and lactic acids** loosen the bonds between dead cells and correct keratinisation around the follicle.
- **Enzyme peels** (papain, bromelain) are the gentle option for a sensitive or irritated scalp.

A 2026 multi-omics study of a combined acid gel on scalp seborrheic dermatitis showed that peeling does more than exfoliate: it rebalanced the scalp microbiota and lipid composition. In practice, itching subsides and any active applied afterwards reaches its target instead of sitting on a crust.

## Step three: HELEO4 light therapy on the scalp

This is the step most Dubai clinics do not offer, and the reason the check-up is built around it. [HELEO4](https://solar-beauty.ae/aesthetic-services/therapy-heleo4) is a photodynamic and LED system: a chlorophyll-based photosensitiser gel is applied to the cleansed scalp, absorbed, and then activated by LED light of a chosen wavelength. Two mechanisms are relevant for the scalp:

1. **Red light (630-660 nm) for the follicle.** This is the wavelength range studied in low-level light therapy for androgenetic alopecia. A meta-analysis of 11 double-blind randomised trials found a significant increase in hair density versus sham devices. In a 24-week trial at 650 nm, three sessions a week, treated areas showed better hair coverage, count and thickness than the untreated side. Red light is absorbed in the mitochondria of dermal papilla cells and is thought to prolong the growth phase and improve microcirculation around the bulb.

2. **Photodynamic action for seborrhea.** When the photosensitiser is activated, it produces reactive oxygen that suppresses Malassezia yeast and reduces sebaceous activity and inflammation. A pilot study using a combined blue and red LED device on seborrheic dermatitis of the scalp reported significant reductions in redness, scaling and itching after 8 weeks, with no serious side effects. Photodynamic therapy is being studied for seborrheic dermatitis for exactly this reason.

A scalp session with HELEO4 lasts about 30-40 minutes, is painless and has no downtime; you wash your hair the next day as usual. How the device works on the face is covered in our article on [LED therapy with HELEO4](https://solar-beauty.ae/blog/led-terapiya-heleo4); on the scalp the logic is the same, only the target is the follicle.

## How the protocol is built

| Situation on trichoscopy | Protocol | Rhythm |

| Diffuse shedding after summer, seasonal | Peel plus HELEO4 red light | 6-8 sessions, then monthly maintenance |

Shedding usually calms within 2-4 weeks; a visible change in density needs 8-12 weeks, the time a new hair takes to grow to a length you can see. Cost depends on the zone and the course and is confirmed at the consultation.

## What to do at home between sessions

- Rinse with filtered or cooler water where possible, and always after a pool.
- Shampoo the scalp, not the lengths; conditioner only from mid-length down.
- Wear a hat on the beach and at outdoor sport: the parting burns first.
- No daily scrubs at home: that is a broken barrier, not a clean scalp.
- Iron, vitamin D and sleep matter more for density than any topical; a blood test is part of a proper hair work-up.

## When you need a trichologist, not a facial for the scalp

A cosmetology check-up is for a healthy or mildly problematic scalp. See a doctor first if you have: shedding of more than about 100 hairs a day for over three months, patchy bald spots, a receding hairline or widening parting, pain, pustules, crusts or scarring, or hair loss after childbirth, illness or a new medication. Trichoscopy at SOLAR flags these cases, and we refer rather than treat.

## FAQ

**Does trichoscopy hurt or require cutting hair?**
No. A digital dermatoscope is placed on the scalp at several points, usually the crown, temples and parting. Nothing is cut or plucked, and the whole examination takes 15-20 minutes. Come with clean hair without oils or styling products.

**Can I do a scalp peel if I colour my hair?**
Yes, but not in the same week. A peel is done on clean, uncoloured skin at least 5-7 days after colouring, and colour is applied no earlier than 3-4 days after the peel, so the scalp barrier has time to recover.

**Is HELEO4 on the scalp safe in Dubai's sun?**
LED light is not UV and does not make the scalp photosensitive the way a chemical peel or laser might. The photosensitiser is removed after the session. We still recommend a hat for the first day, mainly because the freshly exfoliated skin is more vulnerable to the sun.

**How many sessions before I see a difference?**
Itching, flaking and oiliness improve after the first or second session. Shedding reduces in 2-4 weeks. Thicker-looking hair shows after 8-12 weeks, because the hair cycle is slow and new hairs need time to grow out.

**Is this a treatment for baldness?**
No. The protocol supports a healthy scalp and can complement a medical hair loss plan, but androgenetic alopecia is diagnosed and treated by a trichologist or dermatologist. Trichoscopy is the step where we tell one from the other.
